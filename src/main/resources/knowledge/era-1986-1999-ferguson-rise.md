TOPIC: Alex Ferguson's rise — from near-sacking to the Treble (1986–1999)
CATEGORY: era   AS OF: 2026-07   STATUS: stable-history

Alex Ferguson arrived from Aberdeen in November 1986, survived early pressure, ended Manchester United's 26-year league title drought in 1993, and built successive great sides — powered by Eric Cantona and then the "Class of '92" — culminating in the 1999 Treble of Premier League, FA Cup and Champions League.

Alex Ferguson's early years were hard. By December 1989, with results poor, a famous banner at Old Trafford read "Three years of excuses and it's still crap — ta-ra Fergie". Folklore holds that Mark Robins's winner away to Nottingham Forest in the FA Cup third round in January 1990 saved Ferguson's job. Manchester United went on to win the 1990 FA Cup, beating Crystal Palace 1-0 in a replay through Lee Martin's goal — Ferguson's first trophy at the club.

Trophies accelerated: the European Cup Winners' Cup in 1991 (2-1 over Barcelona in Rotterdam, both goals by Mark Hughes) and the League Cup in 1992. Manchester United narrowly lost the 1991-92 title race to Leeds United, but in November 1992 Ferguson signed Leeds' French forward Eric Cantona for about £1.2 million — the transformative transfer of the era. Manchester United won the inaugural Premier League in 1992-93, the club's first championship since 1967, and completed the club's first league-and-FA-Cup Double in 1993-94, with Roy Keane signed from Nottingham Forest for a British record £3.75 million.

In January 1995 Eric Cantona kung-fu kicked a Crystal Palace fan and was banned for eight months. Manchester United lost the 1994-95 title on the final day and the FA Cup final to Everton. In summer 1995 Ferguson controversially sold Paul Ince, Mark Hughes and Andrei Kanchelskis and promoted the "Class of '92" youth graduates: Ryan Giggs, David Beckham, Paul Scholes, Nicky Butt, and Gary and Phil Neville. After an opening-day defeat, pundit Alan Hansen declared "You can't win anything with kids." Manchester United won the Double that season (1995-96), with the returned Cantona scoring the FA Cup final winner against Liverpool, and retained the league in 1996-97. Cantona retired abruptly in May 1997, aged 30, and Roy Keane became captain.

The 1998-99 season produced the Treble, unique in English football. Manchester United won the Premier League by one point over Arsenal, clinched on the final day against Tottenham. Manchester United won the FA Cup, beating Newcastle 2-0 in the final, after Ryan Giggs's legendary extra-time solo goal beat Arsenal in a semi-final replay. And on 26 May 1999 — what would have been Sir Matt Busby's 90th birthday — Manchester United beat Bayern Munich 2-1 in the Champions League final at the Camp Nou, with stoppage-time goals from Teddy Sheringham and Ole Gunnar Solskjaer (see the dedicated Treble final document). Ferguson was knighted that summer.
