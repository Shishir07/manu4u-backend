TOPIC: George Best — Manchester United legend, the "Fifth Beatle" (played 1963–1974)
CATEGORY: legend   AS OF: 2026-07   STATUS: stable-history

George Best was Manchester United's mercurial Northern Irish winger from 1963 to 1974, scorer of 179 goals in 470 appearances, European Cup winner and Ballon d'Or winner in 1968, and football's first pop-culture superstar. His genius was matched by a public battle with alcoholism that ended his top-level career at 27 and his life at 59.

Born in Belfast on 22 May 1946, George Best was spotted at 15 by Manchester United's Northern Ireland scout Bob Bishop, who sent manager Matt Busby the famous telegram: "I think I've found you a genius." Homesick, Best fled back to Belfast after two days but returned, and made his first-team debut in September 1963, aged 17.

Best combined balance, two-footedness, dribbling and courage against brutal tackling. He was leading man in the Best–Law–Charlton "Holy Trinity" that won league titles in 1964-65 and 1966-67. After scoring twice in a 5-1 European Cup quarter-final win away to Benfica in March 1966, the 19-year-old returned in a sombrero and the press christened him "El Beatle" — the moment celebrity football was born. In the 1968 European Cup final against Benfica, Best rounded the goalkeeper in extra time for Manchester United's decisive second goal. He won the Ballon d'Or and was English Football Writers' Footballer of the Year in 1968, and was Manchester United's top scorer for five consecutive seasons.

Signature moments include six goals in an 8-2 FA Cup win at Northampton Town in February 1970 (his first game back from suspension). As the post-Busby team declined, Best's drinking and disappearances worsened; he played his final Manchester United match on 1 January 1974, aged 27, and later drifted through spells including Fulham and the Los Angeles Aztecs. He won 37 caps for Northern Ireland but never played at a World Cup.

Best's wit fed his legend: "I spent a lot of money on booze, birds and fast cars — the rest I just squandered," and the hotel porter anecdote that ends "Mr Best, where did it all go wrong?" delivered as he lay on a bed covered in casino winnings beside Miss World. Pelé is often quoted calling Best the greatest player in the world, and fans coined "Maradona good, Pelé better, George Best."

George Best died on 25 November 2005, aged 59, of multiple organ failure linked to alcoholism, having had a liver transplant in 2002. An estimated 100,000 people lined Belfast's streets for his funeral, and Belfast's airport was renamed George Best Belfast City Airport. At Old Trafford he stands in the "United Trinity" statue alongside Denis Law and Bobby Charlton, facing the stadium.
