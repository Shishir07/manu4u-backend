TOPIC: The Munich air disaster — 6 February 1958
CATEGORY: moment   AS OF: 2026-07   STATUS: stable-history

On 6 February 1958, the aircraft carrying Manchester United home from a European Cup tie crashed on its third takeoff attempt at Munich-Riem airport, killing 23 of the 44 people aboard, including eight Manchester United players. The disaster destroyed the "Busby Babes" team at its peak and remains the defining tragedy in the club's history, commemorated every year on 6 February.

Manchester United were returning from Belgrade, where a 3-3 draw with Red Star Belgrade on 5 February 1958 had sealed a 5-4 aggregate win and a place in the European Cup semi-finals. The chartered British European Airways Elizabethan aircraft, Flight 609, stopped to refuel at Munich in heavy snow. After two aborted takeoff attempts, the third attempt failed — slush on the runway prevented the plane reaching takeoff speed — and the aircraft ploughed through a fence and struck a house. Captain James Thain was long blamed by German investigators for not de-icing the wings, but was eventually cleared; the accepted cause is runway slush.

Eight Manchester United players died: club captain Roger Byrne, Eddie Colman, Mark Jones, David Pegg, Tommy Taylor, Geoff Bent, Liam "Billy" Whelan, and Duncan Edwards, who fought for his life for 15 days before dying on 21 February 1958, aged 21. Duncan Edwards was widely regarded as the greatest young player England had produced. Three club officials died — secretary Walter Crickmer, trainer Tom Curry and coach Bert Whalley — along with eight journalists, including former England goalkeeper Frank Swift.

Manager Matt Busby suffered critical injuries and was twice given the last rites, but survived after two months in a Munich hospital. Goalkeeper Harry Gregg became known as the "Hero of Munich" for returning into the burning wreckage to pull survivors out, including a baby and teammates Bobby Charlton and Dennis Viollet. Winger Johnny Berry and forward Jackie Blanchflower survived but never played again.

Assistant manager Jimmy Murphy had missed the trip because he was managing Wales in a World Cup qualifier. Murphy kept Manchester United running, and an emotional makeshift team reached the 1958 FA Cup final, losing 2-0 to Bolton Wanderers. Matt Busby returned and rebuilt the club, a mission completed when Manchester United won the European Cup on 29 May 1968, with Munich survivors Bobby Charlton and Bill Foulkes in the team.

The disaster is memorialised at Old Trafford by the Munich Clock (permanently showing 6 February 1958, 3:04pm) and the Munich Tunnel memorial, and by the "Flowers of Manchester" — the fans' lament sung on each anniversary.
