TOPIC: The post-Ferguson era — Moyes to Amorim, INEOS, and the long rebuild (2013–2026)
CATEGORY: era   AS OF: 2026-07   STATUS: stable-history (verified July 2026; ongoing events tracked in topical- documents)

Since Sir Alex Ferguson retired in 2013, Manchester United have cycled through David Moyes, Louis van Gaal, Jose Mourinho, Ole Gunnar Solskjaer, Erik ten Hag and Ruben Amorim without winning a Premier League title; club legend Michael Carrick became head coach in January 2026 and led the club back into the Champions League with a 3rd-place finish in 2025-26. The era brought cup successes (FA Cups 2016 and 2024, League Cups 2017 and 2023, Europa League 2017) but chronic underachievement, and since February 2024 football operations have been controlled by Sir Jim Ratcliffe's INEOS as minority owner.

David Moyes, Ferguson's chosen successor, was sacked after ten months in April 2014 with Manchester United seventh. Louis van Gaal (2014-2016) won the 2016 FA Cup — Jesse Lingard's extra-time winner beat Crystal Palace 3-2 — but was sacked days later. Jose Mourinho (2016-2018) delivered the 2017 League Cup and the 2017 Europa League (2-0 over Ajax in Stockholm, goals by Paul Pogba and Henrikh Mkhitaryan), plus a second-place league finish in 2017-18, but was sacked in December 2018. Paul Pogba's £89 million world-record return from Juventus in 2016 symbolised the era's expensive, uneven recruitment.

Club legend Ole Gunnar Solskjaer (December 2018 – November 2021) provided the dramatic 3-1 comeback win at Paris Saint-Germain in March 2019 and a second-place finish in 2020-21, but lost the 2021 Europa League final to Villarreal 11-10 on penalties. Cristiano Ronaldo's emotional return in summer 2021 could not save Solskjaer, sacked after a 4-1 defeat at Watford in November 2021; Ralf Rangnick served as interim.

Erik ten Hag (2022-2024) won the 2023 League Cup (2-0 over Newcastle, ending a six-year trophy drought) and the 2024 FA Cup — a 2-1 upset of Manchester City through teenagers Alejandro Garnacho and Kobbie Mainoo — but league form collapsed: eighth place in 2023-24, Manchester United's then-worst Premier League finish. Ten Hag was sacked in October 2024; Ruud van Nistelrooy served as interim before Ruben Amorim arrived from Sporting Lisbon in November 2024.

Ruben Amorim's 3-4-2-1 rebuild began disastrously: Manchester United finished 15th in the 2024-25 Premier League, the club's worst top-flight finish since the relegation season of 1973-74, and lost the May 2025 Europa League final 1-0 to Tottenham in Bilbao, missing Champions League qualification. Summer 2025 recruitment reshaped the attack: Matheus Cunha (from Wolves), Bryan Mbeumo (from Brentford) and striker Benjamin Sesko (from RB Leipzig) arrived. Ruben Amorim was sacked in January 2026, midway through the 2025-26 season.

Michael Carrick — the former Manchester United midfielder (2006-2018) who won five Premier League titles and the 2008 Champions League as a player, and who had served as caretaker manager for three matches in December 2021 — was announced on 13 January 2026 as short-term head coach for the remainder of the 2025-26 season. On 22 May 2026, Michael Carrick was confirmed as Manchester United's permanent head coach on a long-term contract running to 2028, after winning 11 of his 16 matches in charge. Under Michael Carrick, Manchester United surged to a 3rd-place finish in the 2025-26 Premier League with 71 points (20 wins, 11 draws, 7 defeats), returning to the Champions League for 2026-27. The domestic cups had already brought embarrassment earlier in the season under Ruben Amorim: a Carabao Cup second-round exit on penalties to League Two side Grimsby Town, and an FA Cup third-round 2-1 home defeat to Brighton. Michael Carrick's first transfer window, in summer 2026, brought in midfielders Andrey Santos (from Chelsea) and Youri Tielemans (from Aston Villa), while Casemiro left on a free transfer and Rasmus Højlund moved permanently to Napoli.

Off the pitch, Sir Jim Ratcliffe's INEOS completed the purchase of a minority stake (initially about 27.7%) in February 2024, taking control of football operations from the Glazer family, followed by heavy cost-cutting and executive churn.
