TOPIC: The 1999 Champions League final and the Treble — Camp Nou, 26 May 1999
CATEGORY: moment   AS OF: 2026-07   STATUS: stable-history

On 26 May 1999 at the Camp Nou in Barcelona, Manchester United beat Bayern Munich 2-1 in the Champions League final with two goals in stoppage time, completing the Treble — Premier League, FA Cup and European Cup in a single season — a feat no other English men's club has achieved.

Manchester United trailed from the sixth minute, when Mario Basler's free kick put Bayern Munich ahead. Bayern hit the woodwork twice late on through Mehmet Scholl and Carsten Jancker, and UEFA officials had reportedly tied Bayern's ribbons to the trophy. Manchester United were without suspended captain Roy Keane and midfielder Paul Scholes, and Alex Ferguson gambled by sending on substitutes Teddy Sheringham and Ole Gunnar Solskjaer.

In the first minute of stoppage time, David Beckham's corner was helped on by goalkeeper Peter Schmeichel — up for the set piece in his final Manchester United match — and Ryan Giggs's miscued shot was turned in by Teddy Sheringham. Two minutes later, from another Beckham corner, Sheringham flicked on and Ole Gunnar Solskjaer stabbed the ball into the roof of the net. Commentator Clive Tyldesley's line "name on the trophy" and Alex Ferguson's post-match "Football, bloody hell" became part of club folklore. The date, 26 May, would have been Sir Matt Busby's 90th birthday.

The Treble's other legs were dramatic in their own right. Manchester United won the 1998-99 Premier League by a single point over Arsenal, clinching it on the final day with a comeback 2-1 win over Tottenham. The FA Cup run included the epic semi-final replay against Arsenal at Villa Park: Peter Schmeichel saved Dennis Bergkamp's last-minute penalty, and Ryan Giggs scored one of the FA Cup's greatest goals — a slaloming solo run through Arsenal's defence in extra time — before United beat Newcastle 2-0 in the final. The Champions League run featured a 3-2 semi-final second-leg win away to Juventus, in which captain Roy Keane, booked early (ruling him out of the final), dragged the team back from 2-0 down with a header and what Ferguson called the most selfless midfield performance he had seen.

Key figures of the Treble season: the strike partnership of Dwight Yorke and Andy Cole, wingers David Beckham and Ryan Giggs, midfielders Roy Keane and Paul Scholes, defenders Jaap Stam and Gary Neville, and goalkeeper Peter Schmeichel, who lifted the European Cup as captain in his farewell game. Alex Ferguson was knighted weeks later.
