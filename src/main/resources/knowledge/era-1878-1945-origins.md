TOPIC: Manchester United origins — Newton Heath, the 1902 rescue, and the pre-war years (1878–1945)
CATEGORY: era   AS OF: 2026-07   STATUS: stable-history

Manchester United began in 1878 as Newton Heath LYR, a works team founded by railway workers of the Lancashire and Yorkshire Railway's Carriage and Wagon department. The club nearly died of debt in 1902, was rescued and renamed Manchester United, and won its first major trophies before the First World War.

Newton Heath LYR played in green and gold — the colours later revived by fan protests against the Glazer family in 2010. Newton Heath joined the Football League in 1892 but struggled on the pitch and financially throughout the 1890s.

By early 1902 Newton Heath owed around £2,670 and faced a winding-up order. Club captain Harry Stafford famously raised rescue funds — legend says his St Bernard dog helped attract the attention of local brewery owner John Henry Davies. Davies and a group of businessmen paid off the debts, took over the club, and on 24 April 1902 renamed it Manchester United, rejecting alternative names including Manchester Central and Manchester Celtic. The club also adopted red shirts.

Under manager Ernest Mangnall, Manchester United won their first Football League championship in the 1907-08 season. Manchester United won the FA Cup for the first time in 1909, beating Bristol City 1-0 with a goal by Sandy Turnbull. A second league title followed in 1910-11. The star of that first great side was Welsh winger Billy Meredith, one of football's first celebrity players and a founder of the players' union.

Manchester United moved into Old Trafford in February 1910, a stadium built with John Henry Davies's money and designed by the engineer Archibald Leitch. The first match at Old Trafford, on 19 February 1910, was a 4-3 defeat to Liverpool.

Between the two world wars Manchester United declined badly. The club yo-yoed between the First and Second Divisions, and by 1931 was again close to bankruptcy. A second rescuer, clothing manufacturer James W. Gibson, took over in December 1931, stabilised the finances, and later backed the youth structures (the Manchester United Junior Athletic Club, founded 1938) that would feed the post-war revolution.

During the Second World War, Old Trafford was heavily damaged by German bombing on 11 March 1941, aimed at the adjacent Trafford Park industrial estate. Manchester United played home matches at Manchester City's Maine Road until Old Trafford reopened in 1949. It was into this bombed-out, trophy-starved club that Matt Busby arrived as manager in October 1945.
