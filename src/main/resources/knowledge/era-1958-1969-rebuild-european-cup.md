TOPIC: Post-Munich rebuild to the 1968 European Cup — Best, Law and Charlton (1958–1969)
CATEGORY: era   AS OF: 2026-07   STATUS: stable-history

In the decade after the Munich air disaster, Matt Busby and Jimmy Murphy rebuilt Manchester United around the "Holy Trinity" of George Best, Denis Law and Bobby Charlton, winning the FA Cup in 1963, league titles in 1965 and 1967, and — ten years and three months after Munich — the European Cup in 1968, the first won by an English club.

Immediately after Munich, with Matt Busby critically injured in hospital, assistant Jimmy Murphy kept the club running with a patched-together team of survivors, reserves and emergency signings. That improvised side reached the 1958 FA Cup final, losing 2-0 to Bolton Wanderers, in one of English football's most emotional occasions.

Busby rebuilt through a blend of youth and signings. Munich survivor Bobby Charlton became the team's heartbeat. Busby signed Scottish forward Denis Law from Torino in 1962 for a British record £115,000, and winger Paddy Crerand. From the youth system came Belfast-born George Best, who debuted in September 1963 at age 17. Manchester United won the FA Cup in 1963, beating Leicester City 3-1, then won the First Division title in 1964-65 and again in 1966-67.

The Best–Law–Charlton trident made Manchester United the most glamorous team in England. Uniquely, all three won the Ballon d'Or: Denis Law in 1964, Bobby Charlton in 1966 (the year he also won the World Cup with England), and George Best in 1968.

The 1968 European Cup triumph was the culmination of Busby's post-Munich mission. In the semi-final, Manchester United beat Real Madrid 4-3 on aggregate, surviving a 3-3 second leg in Madrid with a goal from Munich survivor Bill Foulkes. In the final at Wembley on 29 May 1968, Manchester United beat Benfica 4-1 after extra time: Bobby Charlton scored twice, George Best rounded the goalkeeper for the second, and 19-year-old Brian Kidd scored on his birthday. Busby, who had twice been given the last rites at Munich, and Charlton, who survived the crash, embraced on the pitch. Manchester United were the first English club — and second British, after Celtic in 1967 — to win the European Cup.

Matt Busby was knighted in 1968 and retired as manager in 1969 after 24 years (briefly returning as caretaker in 1970-71). The team aged quickly and the club entered a long decline; Best's career unravelled amid alcoholism and he played his last Manchester United match in January 1974, aged 27.
