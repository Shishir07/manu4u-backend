TOPIC: Matt Busby's first era and the Busby Babes (1945–1958)
CATEGORY: era   AS OF: 2026-07   STATUS: stable-history

Matt Busby became Manchester United manager in October 1945 and, with assistant Jimmy Murphy, built two great teams in a decade: the 1948 FA Cup winners, and then the "Busby Babes" — a home-grown side of prodigies that won back-to-back league titles and pioneered English participation in Europe before being destroyed by the Munich air disaster in February 1958.

Matt Busby, a former Manchester City and Liverpool player, demanded unprecedented control over team affairs when he took the Manchester United job — effectively inventing the modern "manager" role in England. His Welsh assistant Jimmy Murphy ran youth development and was the hands-on builder of the youth pipeline.

Busby's first side won the FA Cup in 1948, beating Blackpool 4-2 at Wembley in a final regarded as a classic, and won the First Division title in 1951-52 — Manchester United's first league championship in 41 years. That team was ageing, and Busby made a radical choice: rather than buy replacements, he promoted teenagers from the youth system.

The resulting side was nicknamed the "Busby Babes". With an average age of around 22, the Busby Babes won the First Division title in 1955-56 by an 11-point margin, and retained it in 1956-57. The team's jewel was Duncan Edwards, a colossal half-back who debuted at 16 and was widely considered the finest young player England had ever produced. Other key Busby Babes included captain Roger Byrne, forward Tommy Taylor, wing-half Eddie Colman, and a young Bobby Charlton.

Manchester United became the first English club to enter the European Cup, in the 1956-57 season. The Football League had pressured Chelsea not to enter in 1955; Busby defied similar pressure because he believed Europe was football's future. Manchester United beat Anderlecht 10-0 in an early home tie (played at Maine Road, as Old Trafford's floodlights were not ready) and reached the semi-finals, losing to Real Madrid. In 1957-58, Manchester United again reached the European Cup quarter-finals — and it was returning from that tie, a 3-3 draw away to Red Star Belgrade on 5 February 1958 that sealed a 5-4 aggregate win, that the team's plane crashed at Munich the following day.

The Munich air disaster of 6 February 1958 killed 23 people, including eight Manchester United players. (See the dedicated Munich air disaster document for full detail.) It ended the Busby Babes era at its peak: the team had been chasing a third straight title and was among the favourites for the European Cup.
